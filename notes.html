As we all know, a plane R can be expressed as a unit surface normal n times the (signed) distance a along n from the origin to the plane:
n ⋅ R = a .

Points R are "inside" the plane if they satisfy:
n ⋅ R < a .

We're going to expand this to include spheres. The interior R of a given sphere can be represented by 2 diametrically opposed surface points P:
( P0 −R) ⋅ ( P1 −R) <0 ,

Let's first constrain the points P such that they are colinear with the origin. Then, we can express each point in P as the signed distance from the origin times the unit surface normal at the corresponding point. Since the points are diametrically opposed on a sphere, the normals are opposite each other, and we can write them both in terms of the normal n at P0, with distances from the origin a and b:
P0 = an P1 = -bn
Substituting, distributing, and expanding gives:
R⋅R −R⋅an +R⋅bn −ab <0 .
Multiplying both sides by 1/b and taking the limit b → ∞ for each, we obtain:
R⋅R b →0
R⋅(an) b →0
R⋅(bn) b →R⋅n
-abb →-a
We then observe the limiting result as b goes to infinity:
R⋅n −a<0 ,
is our original plane equation:
n⋅R<a ,
so we have a uniform way to express spheres and planes. However, representing a plane this way requires infinite values for b.
To avoid the need for infinite values, let's reformulate in terms of curvature k:
k = 1 | a + b |
Note that |a + b| is the Euclidean diameter of the sphere.
If we rewrite our unifying inequality thus:
R⋅R + ( b−a ) ( R⋅n ) − ab =0
... we can use our definition of curvature k:
b = 1 k − a ∴ b −a = 1 k − 2a and −ab = −a k + a2
... to eliminate b:
R⋅R + ( 1 k − 2a ) ( R⋅n ) − a k + a2 <0 ,
... and because k ≥ 0, we can multiply both sides by k and eliminate the singularity at k = 0. So, our final unifying formula is:
kR⋅R + ( 1 − 2ak ) ( R⋅n ) − a + ka2 <0
Thus, we have a unifying formula for planes and spheres in n, a, and k.

Planes are simply spheres with k = 0.

This works in any dimension.
