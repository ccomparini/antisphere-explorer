A plane comprised of the set of points R can be represented in point-normal form using 2 vectors - an arbitrary point R0 on that plane, and a vector n normal to that plane using the equation:
n ⋅ ( R − R0 ) = 0 .

If we constrain the choice of R0 such that it's on the line through the origin in the direction of the normal vector, we can write:
n ⋅ ( R − an ) = 0
where a is the distance from the origin along the line in the direction of n.

Expanding, we have:
n ⋅ R − a ( n ⋅ n ) =0 .

Now, if we also constrain n to be a unit vector, then n⋅n = 1, and we obtain:
n ⋅ R = a .

Based on this extremely simple and useful formula, points can be said to be in the subspace "inside" the plane if:
n ⋅ R < a .

Thus, planes can be encoded compactly and conveniently in 2 components: { n, a }.

Enough introduction. Say we wish to extend this encoding to spheres. Points R are within a sphere defined by 2 diametrically opposed points P where P⊆R iff:
( P0 −R) ⋅ ( P1 −R) <0 ,

Now, for our representation, let us constrain the points P such that they are colinear with the origin at distances a and b. Let n be a unit vector pointing toward the origin of the sphere along that line. So:
P0 = an P1 = bn

Substituting, distributing, and expanding gives:
R⋅R −R⋅an −R⋅bn +ab <0 .
If we divide the separate components by b and take the limit b → ∞ for each, we obtain:
R⋅R b →0
R⋅(an) b →0
R⋅(bn) b →R⋅n
abb →a
We then observe the limiting result as b goes to infinity is our original plane equation:
−R⋅n +a<0 , i.e., R⋅n>a but with the normal in the wrong direction? grrr

Now we have a uniform encoding for spheres and planes, wherein planes are just spheres with one of 2 diametric points at an infinite distance along a diametric line throught the origin.
